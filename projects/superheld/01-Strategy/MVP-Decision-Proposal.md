# MVP Decision Proposal

## Recommendation Summary

This is the recommended decision set for the first Superheld MVP cycle:

1. target families and households with children first
2. lead with family safety, guided recovery, guided learning, and authenticity support
3. lock five protection cases for the MVP
4. start with one narrow social-message, fake-profile, and authenticity-risk slice
5. keep household approvals and learning recommendations in the product surface from the start

## Recommended First Audience

Primary audience:

- families
- parents or guardians
- households that need help around social media, spam, scams, and safe online habits

Why this audience first:

- the child and family safety problem is clear and urgent
- social-media-related scams and spam are easy to explain and validate with users
- learning and approvals are naturally household features
- the product can deliver value even before deep endpoint protection exists

Secondary audience:

- individual users with high scam and phishing exposure

## Recommended Launch Promise

Superheld should launch with a guided recovery promise:

"When a suspicious message, fake profile, deepfake, link, or account situation puts your family at risk, Superheld explains what matters, what to do next, and what to learn from it."

Why this promise:

- it is credible for an MVP
- it differentiates from generic antivirus language
- it fits deterministic playbooks, family approvals, and learning center follow-up

## Recommended MVP Protection Cases

### 1. Suspicious social messages, unknown contacts, and spam risk

- assess suspicious direct messages, contact requests, spam patterns, and social-media-linked scams
- create a single incident with clear evidence
- offer review, family notification, and safe guidance

### 2. Fake account warnings, platform impersonation, and scam links

- classify fake Meta, Instagram, TikTok, bank, or platform-support warnings alongside risky links
- guide the user into safe next actions
- connect to identity checks and account recovery when needed

### 3. Fake profiles, impersonation, and deepfake authenticity risk

- review suspicious profiles, celebrity clones, authority impersonation, and manipulated media
- guide evidence preservation, reporting, blocking, and household-safe next actions
- keep authenticity support honest: bounded review and response, not magic detection claims

### 4. Infostealer fallout and account recovery

- treat suspected credential theft as a recovery workflow
- guide the user through session review, password resets, and MFA checks
- keep the flow deterministic and checklist-driven

### 5. Household alerts, approvals, and learning recommendations

- support approval-gated actions for shared device oversight
- make incidents understandable to a non-expert household owner
- recommend the right learning module after relevant incidents
- provide parent and child variants where useful
- keep the material short, practical, and linked to real household risk

## What Should Not Be in the MVP

- deep Linux endpoint coverage
- deep endpoint parity across desktop platforms on day one
- deep platform control over social networks
- autonomous risky remediation
- "perfect deepfake detector" claims
- broad ransomware ambitions
- generic "protect everything" claims

## Recommended Sequence of Validation

1. prove suspicious social-message, fake-profile, and fake-account-warning handling end to end
2. add household explanation quality, approvals, and parent-child UX
3. add bounded media-authenticity review, reporting guidance, and learning-center recommendations
4. add phishing recovery and account-recovery flows
5. broaden platform or deeper endpoint coverage only after signal quality is good

## Decision To Lock Next

If no stronger constraint appears, this proposal should be treated as the default planning baseline.
